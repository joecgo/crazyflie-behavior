# Backlog #68: Sideways drift during the initial climb

The row's history, moved from the [decision backlog](../decision-backlog.md) on 2026-11-16. The row keeps the open question.

**Sideways drift during the initial climb.** Failsafe high and Hover moved 20-26 cm right by the estimate (Joe saw 18 and 20 cm), nearly all of it during the initial climb: a push of about 2-4 cm as the motors spin up, then a steady 0.1 m/s while roll and pitch are held level, which nothing corrects. The high-level climb then holds the new x and y. `SETTLE_SPEED` (0.15 m/s) counts 0.1 m/s as settled. Blocks the 3 cm landing goal. Options to weigh: a shorter level part, holding position sooner, a lower settle speed, or finding the push at spin-up (floor, propellers). **Session 3 tests steady climb speeds** ([ADR-0059](../decisions/0059-initial-climb-speed-set-per-hover-run.md), 2026-11-09): the hover program's `--climb-speed` (0.1-0.4 m/s, for example 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4, about 3 climbs each) compares the push at spin-up with the drift after it. A slow-then-fast climb waits for that data. **First flight 3 (analysis, 2026-11-10):** inconclusive on speed, because the firmware's thrust base made the slow climbs stall near the floor ([ADR-0061](../decisions/0061-thrust-base-set-at-connect.md) sets it; fly the comparison again after it). The two 0.4 m/s cap landings were real drift, not bad flow: a tilt of about 0.5-1° right and forward after lift-off gave 0.1-0.2 m/s that the level climb never brakes, so the speed never settled under `SETTLE_SPEED`; the cap can't tell drift from bad flow. Every landing with a direction noted was forward and/or right (session 2: right). **Take-off torque (analysis, 2026-11-12):** on 2026-11-09 the motors cancelled a steady front-right torque in every flight (as a centre-of-mass offset, 0.5-1.7 mm right and 0-1.0 mm forward; 11-07 and 11-08 under 0.5 mm), with a 0.4-0.9° right and nose-down tilt in the first 0.5 s at 0.2-0.4 m/s. Joe moved the battery between 11-08 and 11-09: left and back to counter the drift, or centred after an earlier left-back counterbalance; he isn't sure which. From 11-08 to every 11-09 flight the balance moved toward the front-right corner, about equally in roll and pitch (along the M3-M1 diagonal), which fits centring a battery that sat left-back, not moving it further left-back. If so, this drone pushes front-right with the battery centred, and the 11-07/11-08 counterbalance cancelled it (under 0.5 mm). The yaw balance also changed, which a weight shift can't do. **Joe (2026-11-12), asked what else changed:** he moved the battery cable a little further down, further from the propellers. 11-07 and 11-08 still drifted right without that torque, so it isn't the only cause. **Joe's idea (2026-11-12):** the battery can't go in exactly the same spot every time, so correct the drift once the take-off has finished instead of trying to prevent it. Also Joe's: take off inside a lit tube that steadies the drone until it's clear. Other options raised: a lit, textured take-off pad (flow only, no walls); spinning up on the floor before lift-off so the attitude integral settles first (the two 0.1 m/s climbs spent about 2.8 s spinning up and showed no tilt at lift-off; risk: integral windup while the feet hold the drone). **First flight 4 (analysis, 2026-11-13):** both failsafe flights (old M1 propeller) needed a steady front-right correction (rate integrals up to -3,200 roll, +2,800 pitch in Failsafe high) and tilted 0.9-1.9° for about 0.8 s; Failsafe high's real sideways speed (good flow) never settled, and Joe measured 59 cm. The four flights after the M1 propeller swap needed integrals 3-6 times smaller, and their logged tilt gives only 1-5 cm in the level climb. The swap or a battery shift; two flights against four can't tell. Analysis: `drafts/ff4/findings.md`. **Balance and sideways movement (analysis, 2026-11-13):** with the old propeller M1 alone ran high (34% of the thrust command against M3's 31%), which fits a weak propeller better than a shifted battery (#83). After the swap the drone still left the initial climb at 0.04-0.11 m/s in good flow, each time partly to the right, and the high-level climb's position hold stopped it within 2-4 cm. By the position estimate, corrected for the flow model's 0.1 m height floor, the take-off moved the drone 2.5-6 cm and the landing 2-6 cm; Joe's tape and the logs' total differ by about 5-12 cm per flight, because the logs see little of the first and last 10 cm of height (table in [First flight 4](../field-testing/sessions/2026-11-12-first-flight-4.md)). While the drone is held level, its speed estimate gained 0.02-0.11 m/s² to the right that the logged tilt doesn't show, in all 6 level stretches: a level error of up to about 0.75° in the attitude estimate after the reset, or a real push near the floor. To tell a cause on the drone from one in the room: fly with the drone turned 180° on the mark.
