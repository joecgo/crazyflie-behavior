# Backlog #8: Safety-layer severity levels

The row's history, moved from the [decision backlog](../decision-backlog.md) on 2026-11-16. The row keeps the open question.

**Safety-layer severity levels** (for First flight: one failsafe, land; [ADR-0030](../decisions/0030-minimal-safety-layer-for-first-flight.md)): which faults are minor (blend back to steering mode), serious (stop and hover) or critical (land or e-stop), and the response for each. Include **ceiling protection**: a geofence maximum altitude (position estimate z, with a drift margin) plus a `ranges.up` check, with a generous margin for the ceiling effect (airflow pulls a drone toward a nearby ceiling). Relevant for the attic. Decide the **maximum take-off height** in `judge()` with it, taken from the geofence (my choice, 2026-11-02, over a fixed range now): until then nothing caps it but the hover test's 0.5 m constant, and the adapter's `ValueError` for a height of 0 or less comes after approval, so it's an exception rather than a refusal. Include **flight state from the drone, not from what was sent**: the Crazyflie adapter marks itself airborne as soon as it sends a take-off, but a drone that isn't armed refuses it; the safety layer should use the drone status (`is_armed`, `is_flying`) instead. [ADR-0060](../decisions/0060-pre-check-flow-while-the-drone-may-be-flying.md) went the other way for one check: the safety pre-check's velocity uncertainty check uses the adapter's "may be flying" from the snapshot, because the status says flying for 2 s after the motors stop. Deciding this revisits it. Include **take-off right after touchdown**: the take-off rule still uses the drone status, which says flying for 2 s after the motors stop, so a take-off then is refused (safe; [ADR-0039](../decisions/0039-the-adapter-decides-may-be-flying.md) decided who decides "may be flying" for hover and land). Later improvement to landing ([ADR-0019](../decisions/0019-final-descent-when-touchdown-unconfirmed.md) option C): confirm touchdown from several independent signals (no vertical movement, low motor power) once bench data shows what landing looks like in the telemetry.
