# Backlog #19: Player tracking with UWB radio tags

The row's history, moved from the [decision backlog](../decision-backlog.md) on 2026-11-16. The row keeps the open question.

**Parked ([ADR-0015](../decisions/0015-first-game-catch-the-bird.md)).** **Player tracking with UWB radio tags (Bitcraze Loco): the chosen direction.** The player wears a small UWB node (e.g. on a hat; the head is also the best spot for the signal, since bodies block UWB), and the drone measures where the player is to about 10 cm, in any direction, including behind it. With anchors around the room, the drone also gets an absolute position, which removes drift. **Answered from the firmware source (2026-10-20):** a node in tag mode only measures distances and prints them as text over USB (lps-node-firmware `uwb_twr_tag.c`), so a worn tag can't report anything wirelessly without custom firmware. The reverse works with stock firmware: the player wears a node in **anchor** mode with no position configured; the drone's Loco deck measures the distance to it and logs it as `ranging.distanceN`, and because that anchor broadcasts no position, the drone's own position estimate ignores it (crazyflie-firmware `lpsTwrTag.c`; lps-node-firmware `uwb_twr_anchor.c`). That gives drone-to-player distance, not direction: direction would come from moving and re-measuring, or from the range sensors. **Still to check before buying:** (2) full positioning with anchors (setup per room) or drone-to-player distance only (no anchors), and whether cflib exposes the distance-only mode; (3) answered (Bitcraze compatibility matrix): Flow, Multi-ranger and Loco are all compatible with standard firmware. Flow underneath, Multi-ranger on top, Loco on either side; three decks need a longer pin-header set. About 7-8 g of decks against a 15 g maximum payload (the Crazyflie weighs 29 g); flight time shorter than the stock 7 minutes, to be measured. The AI deck (camera) works with Loco only with a patch or workaround, so UWB effectively rules out the onboard camera; (4) current prices for anchors and nodes. **Node facts (Bitcraze datasheet):** a bare 65 x 40 x 13 mm board, 13.3 g, powered by micro-USB or 5-12 V, drawing up to 180 mA, tested range 10 m; currently out of stock. A wearable is the node plus a power bank (often 50-100 g) in a sealed enclosure: likely 100 g or more, heavy for a toddler's hat. A small backpack with the node near the shoulders is probably more practical, and keeps it high for signal. **Safety:** a sealed, child-proof enclosure (the board has exposed connectors); a commercial power bank with protection circuits, never bare cells or coin cells; no cables or cords between parts; supervised play only.
