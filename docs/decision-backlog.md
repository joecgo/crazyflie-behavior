one is decided it becomes an ADR in [`decisions/`](decisions/) and is removed
from this list. The ADRs keep their follow-up notes as history; this file is

| 19 | **Parked ([ADR-0015](decisions/0015-first-game-catch-the-bird.md)).** **Player tracking with UWB radio tags (Bitcraze Loco): the chosen direction.** The player wears a small UWB node (e.g. on a hat; the head is also the best spot for the signal, since bodies block UWB), and the drone measures where the player is to about 10 cm, in any direction, including behind it. With anchors around the room, the drone also gets an absolute position, which removes drift. **Answered from the firmware source (2026-10-20):** a node in tag mode only measures distances and prints them as text over USB (lps-node-firmware `uwb_twr_tag.c`), so a worn tag can't report anything wirelessly without custom firmware. The reverse works with stock firmware: the player wears a node in **anchor** mode with no position configured; the drone's Loco deck measures the distance to it and logs it as `ranging.distanceN`, and because that anchor broadcasts no position, the drone's own position estimate ignores it (crazyflie-firmware `lpsTwrTag.c`; lps-node-firmware `uwb_twr_anchor.c`). That gives drone-to-player distance, not direction: direction would come from moving and re-measuring, or from the range sensors. **Still to check before buying:** (2) full positioning with anchors (setup per room) or drone-to-player distance only (no anchors), and whether cflib exposes the distance-only mode; (3) answered (Bitcraze compatibility matrix): Flow, Multi-ranger and Loco are all compatible with standard firmware. Flow underneath, Multi-ranger on top, Loco on either side; three decks need a longer pin-header set. About 7-8 g of decks against a 15 g maximum payload (the Crazyflie weighs 29 g); flight time shorter than the stock 7 minutes, to be measured. The AI deck (camera) works with Loco only with a patch or workaround, so UWB effectively rules out the onboard camera; (4) current prices for anchors and nodes. **Node facts (Bitcraze datasheet):** a bare 65 x 40 x 13 mm board, 13.3 g, powered by micro-USB or 5-12 V, drawing up to 180 mA, tested range 10 m; currently out of stock. A wearable is the node plus a power bank (often 50-100 g) in a sealed enclosure: likely 100 g or more, heavy for a toddler's hat. A small backpack with the node near the shoulders is probably more practical, and keeps it high for signal. **Safety:** a sealed, child-proof enclosure (the board has exposed connectors); a commercial power bank with protection circuits, never bare cells or coin cells; no cables or cords between parts; supervised play only. | Conversation, 2026-10-20 | Before buying tracking hardware |

| 26 | **A hung tick drops the drone** (found writing [ADR-0024](decisions/0024-tick-timing-strategy.md)). The tick loop can't interrupt a tick that hangs, so setpoints stop. The only backup is the firmware's setpoint timeout: in steering mode it stops following after about 0.5 s and cuts the motors after about 2 s (placeholders, [ADR-0011](decisions/0011-mock-models-setpoint-timeout.md)), so the drone drops. **Checked in the firmware source (2026-10-29):** the two timeouts are 500 ms and 2000 ms (`supervisor.c`), and the 2000 ms one sends the supervisor to its free-fall state. A hang that long is realistic: a cflib send can block up to 2 s during radio trouble ([ADR-0027](decisions/0027-operator-controls.md)). A watchdog thread on the PC is no real backup, because its land command waits behind the same blocked send. Options to explore: an onboard reflex that lands on link or setpoint loss (#12), the firmware's own timeout behavior if it can land instead of cutting, and keeping blocking calls out of the tick. | Conversation, 2026-10-27 | Before the first flight in steering mode |

| 29 | **Connecting has no time limit** (found in code review, 2026-10-29). Checked in cflib's source: connecting has three waits with no timeout, and on Windows Python 3.12 can't interrupt them with Ctrl+C: (1) `open_link()`, the link and the variable lists (cflib normally fails on its own if no drone answers); (2) `wait_for_params()`, which a link lost halfway could leave waiting forever; (3) `reset_estimator()`, which waits until the position estimate holds steady for 10 readings 500 ms apart: at least 5 s every time, forever if it never settles (for example, the Flow deck can't see the floor). **Options:** A. run the connect sequence on a helper thread with a time limit per step; the main thread waits in 0.1 s slices, so Ctrl+C works even on Windows, and a step past its limit closes the link and refuses to start with a message naming it (AI recommendation; a stuck helper thread is left behind and ends with the program); B. our own versions of the three waits, using cflib's callbacks (copies part of cflib); C. time limits only for waits 2 and 3; D. leave it and document "close the window". **Placeholder limits:** 15 s for the link, 10 s for the parameters, 20 s for the estimate (measure at the bench, #25). **Never:** connecting hangs forever, or a failed connect leaves the radio claimed. **Always eventually:** connected, or refused with a message, within the limits. | Code review, 2026-10-29 | Before field-test stage 0 |
